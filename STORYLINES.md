# Voltbean — three storylines for the new spot

A net-new, fully animated 30 s commercial (range 25–35 s), 1920×1080 at 30 fps, music + sound effects, no voiceover.

**Brand facts kept from the old spot** (these are the only claims on screen):
Voltbean · "Electrified cold brew" · flavor STORM · 1000 mg caffeine per can · "Coffee is dead. Long live voltage." · "Drink at your own risk." · brand inks: voltage yellow `#FFE600`, chaos pink `#FF1F7A`, ink `#0A0A0A`, arc white `#FAFAFA`.

**The one thing to remember (viewer's words):** "One can of that stuff and Monday doesn't stand a chance."

**What the old spot was:** 13 hard-cut kinetic-type slides on a black void with glitch, flash and light-leak overlays. It used every default the new HyperFrames rules ban: a dark void, glow, docked logo, every scene entering differently with no cause. No character, no world and no sound tied it together.

## A — "Bean Me Up" (recommended)

*Recommended because a mascot gives every cut a cause and leaves Voltbean with a character it can reuse in every future ad.*

**Style:** 1930s rubber-hose cel animation (bendy limbs, pie-cut eyes, white gloves), boiled line at 12 fps, color-modernized to the brand inks.
**Device + form:** a character does the work · object-to-object chain (every shot ends on the thing that opens the next).

A droopy little coffee bean, Beanie, tries to start Monday and fails comically. His weak mug literally melts. He cracks a Voltbean can, gets struck by his own lightning bolt, and turns into a charged-up hero who blasts through the office in one chain of gags until he lands on the can as the end card.

- **First 2 s:** extreme close-up of Beanie's alarm clock ringing so hard it hops across the nightstand; a limp gloved hand flops onto it and the clock squashes flat. Frame 0 is the bouncing clock on a yellow ground.
- **Beats:** alarm squash → Beanie drags to the kitchen, weak coffee mug sighs and melts into a puddle → the puddle reflects a Voltbean can → *crack* → lightning arcs up his arm, hair stands up, eyes go spiral then sharp → he zips through the office: inbox tower toppled, stairs taken as a slide → "1000 MG" stamped on the can as he high-fives it → he leaps into the can's logo, which becomes the end card: "Coffee is dead. Long live voltage."
- **Look:** flat yellow `#FFE600` ground, ink `#0A0A0A` 6 px boiling outlines, pink `#FF1F7A` only on the lightning and the payoff; display type Luckiest Guy (via @font-face), stepped 12 fps animation on characters, smooth 30 fps camera.
- **Sound:** bouncy swing/big-band bed around 160 bpm with a brass stab when the can cracks; cartoon foley on every action: clock rattle, slide whistle on the melt, *crack-fsssh*, a zap, a cymbal hit on the end card.

## B — "Tomorrow's Morning, Today!"

**Style:** 1950s mid-century modern limited animation (UPA / atomic-age ads): flat cut shapes, no outlines, offset color blocks, starburst graphics, stylized people with tiny legs.
**Device + form:** life before and after · one continuous sideways pan across a single long set.

A cheerful-ad parody: an "atomic age" household where nothing works because nobody's awake. Dad is ironing the newspaper, the dog is walking itself. The camera trucks right through the house, and halfway it passes a Voltbean can on the kitchen counter; from that frame on, everything is electrified, fast and gleaming.

- **First 2 s:** a flat starburst clock on a pink wall reads 7:04; a stylized man in a tie stands motionless mid-yawn, his mouth one giant flat oval. The camera is already sliding right.
- **Beats:** yawning man → sleepwalking kitchen (toast pops and nobody catches it) → the pan stops on the can, a starburst says "1000 MG!" → one *zap*, colors snap from dusty to full brand inks → the same household now at triple speed (shirt ironed in one stroke, briefcase grabbed mid-air, the car driving off before the door closes) → the pan ends on a giant can in front of a mid-century skyline: "Drink at your own risk."
- **Look:** before half in dusty versions of the inks (`#C9B84A`, `#B8647F`), after half in full `#FFE600` / `#FF1F7A` / `#0A0A0A`; flat shapes with 2% paper grain; display type League Gothic (bundled) plus a hand-lettered script for the starbursts; no gradients.
- **Sound:** sleepy lounge-exotica bed (vibraphone, ~80 bpm) that tape-stops at the can, then the same tune doubles to a frantic swing; effects: toaster pop, a single zap, a "ding!" on the starburst.

## C — "SIP." (the bold one)

**Style:** Japanese anime action, sakuga-style: hard impact frames, speed lines, smear frames, katakana sound effects drawn into the shot.
**Device + form:** the product as the hook · an endless zoom (the camera keeps diving into a detail that turns out to be the next world).

A tired office worker takes one sip and the film treats it like a shonen power-up. The camera dives into her eye, into the bloodstream, into a single caffeine molecule that cracks open into a storm, then pulls all the way back out to show she's now a legend at her desk.

- **First 2 s:** a hard impact frame: black-and-white inverted silhouette of a hand gripping the can, katakana "ドン" (DON) slamming in. Frame 1 snaps to full color: her exhausted face, two-tone hair, can at her lips.
- **Beats:** the sip as an impact frame → dive into her pupil (lightning reflected) → the bloodstream as a speed-line tunnel → one molecule labelled "1000 MG" splits into a storm (flavor: STORM) → whip back out through her eye → she's transformed: hair crackling, inbox emails ripping out of the screen like cards she slices through → hero pose with the can, the pose freezing into a manga panel → panel shatters into the Voltbean lockup.
- **Look:** cel-shaded two-tone shading, ink `#0A0A0A` line at 4 px, speed lines in yellow `#FFE600` on white `#FAFAFA` impact frames, pink `#FF1F7A` for the storm; display type Archivo Black (bundled) plus Noto Sans JP (bundled) for the drawn sound effects; animation on 2s (15 fps) for drawn parts, 1–2 frame impact frames.
- **Sound:** driving anime-opening rock (~170 bpm) that drops out to near silence for the dive into the eye, then slams back in at the transformation; effects: a whoosh on every zoom, an impact hit on each impact frame, a shatter on the end card.
